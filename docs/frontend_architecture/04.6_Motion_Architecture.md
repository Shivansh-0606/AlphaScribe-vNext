## Purpose

Define the **motion architecture** that delivers the frozen motion design — **communicative, token-governed,
reduced-motion-safe** — consistently, using the **Motion** library. It delivers the frozen
[Motion Specifications](../experience_design/engineering_handoff/05_Motion_Specifications.md) and Constitution
§14; it defines no new animations (those are frozen).

## Scope

**In scope:** motion governance (how motion is centrally controlled), token-driven motion consumption, the
reduced-motion architecture, and enforcement of the "motion must communicate, never decorate" rule. **Out of
scope:** defining/redesigning animations (frozen 23-entry catalogue), and implementation.

## Architectural Decisions

### AD-1 — Motion is governed centrally and delivered via the Motion library
Motion is a **governed infrastructure concern**, not per-component improvisation. It is delivered through
the approved **Motion** library (§5.4), orchestrated from the frozen [Motion Specifications](../experience_design/engineering_handoff/05_Motion_Specifications.md)
(the 23-entry catalogue) — the single source of what animates, when, and how. No animation exists that is
not in the frozen catalogue.

### AD-2 — Motion values come only from tokens
Durations and easings are consumed **only from the frozen motion tokens** (`--motion-duration-*`,
`--motion-ease-*`) via the token layer ([04.2](04.2_Design_Token_Consumption_Strategy.md)); an ad-hoc ms/curve
in a component is a defect (§4.10). One motion vocabulary → coherence and a single reduced-motion switch.

### AD-3 — Reduced motion is a central, lossless capability
A **single reduced-motion signal** (from [04.5](04.5_Accessibility_Architecture.md)) governs all motion:
when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, durations collapse to instant and non-essential animation is removed,
with **no loss of information** (essential progress like determinate bars and progressive streaming
chunks remains) — WCAG 2.3.3, frozen Motion. This is provided once and honored everywhere, not per-component.

### AD-4 — Motion must communicate (the governance rule)
Per Constitution §14 and Direction C, motion is delivered only where it **communicates continuity, feedback,
hierarchy, perceived performance, or AI liveness**; **decorative/ambient motion is prohibited** (§19). If a
motion can be removed without losing meaning, it is not added. The frozen catalogue already encodes this;
the architecture enforces it (the "purpose test").

### AD-5 — Motion is compositor-friendly and non-blocking
Motion is architected to animate **transform/opacity** (compositor-friendly) rather than layout properties,
to **never block interaction**, and to **never shift content the user is reading** ([04.8](04.8_Performance_Architecture.md),
§4.7). AI streaming motion is paced for readability, not speed (§7; [03.13](03.13_AI_Streaming_Lifecycle_Architecture.md)).

### AD-6 — Motion fidelity is enforced
Each animation matches its frozen catalogue entry (trigger/duration/easing/purpose); interruption/reversal
behavior and the reduced-motion fallback are verified in the **Design QA motion pass**
([Design QA](../experience_design/engineering_handoff/10_Design_QA_Process.md)). Motion fidelity and
reduced-motion are acceptance criteria, not niceties.

## Design Rationale

Governing motion centrally — token-driven, catalogue-sourced, with one reduced-motion switch — is how a
premium "calm but alive" experience stays consistent and accessible across the product without motion
sprawling into decoration or breaking for reduced-motion users. Compositor-friendly, non-blocking motion
keeps it performant. This delivers the frozen motion design faithfully and safely.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Per-component ad-hoc animation** — rejected: drift, inconsistency, and reduced-motion gaps (contra §14,
  §4.6); motion is governed centrally.
- **Hardcoded durations/curves** — rejected: breaks coherence and the single reduced-motion switch (contra
  §4.10, [04.2](04.2_Design_Token_Consumption_Strategy.md)).
- **CSS-only animation everywhere** — partially retained for simple transitions, but orchestrated
  enter/exit (overlays, companion, streaming) uses the Motion library per the stack; the token/​reduced-
  motion rules apply to both.

## Trade-offs

- **Motion library vs. CSS-only:** the library adds a dependency but gives orchestrated enter/exit and
  interruption control the frozen motion needs; simple transitions can still be CSS. Accepted (stack-approved).
- **Central governance vs. local expressiveness:** disallowing bespoke motion limits local flourish, but
  that is precisely what the anti-decoration rule requires (§19). Accepted.

## Risks

- **Decorative/ambient motion** creeping in. *Mitigation:* purpose test (AD-4) + Design QA motion pass.
- **Reduced-motion information loss.** *Mitigation:* central lossless reduced-motion (AD-3); QA verifies.
- **Janky/layout-shifting animation.** *Mitigation:* transform/opacity + non-blocking rule (AD-5).

## Future Extension Points

- New animations (added by design via CR to the frozen Motion spec) flow through the same token-governed,
  reduced-motion-safe delivery (§4.14).
- Motion tuning constants (e.g. streaming pace) are exposed as configuration, tunable without redesign.
- Multi-turn AI motion (future roadmap) extends the streaming motion under the same governance.
