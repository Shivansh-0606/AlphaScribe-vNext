## Purpose

Define the **architecture that consistently delivers the frozen non-happy-path experiences** — loading,
skeleton, empty, no-results, offline, permission-denied, error, partial-failure, timeout, and success — so
every surface handles them **uniformly, recoverably, and without losing work**. It delivers the frozen
[States](../design/13_States.md) catalogue and applies the [M3 Error & Recovery](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md)
architecture at the experience layer; it defines no new states.

## Scope

**In scope:** the consistent delivery of the frozen States as experience infrastructure — standardized state
rendering, scoping, recovery, and perceived-performance behavior. **Out of scope:** redefining the States
(frozen), the data/error mechanics ([M3 03.11](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md)/[03.4](03.4_Data_Fetching_Strategy.md)),
and implementation.

## Dependencies

[Constitution](01_Frontend_Architecture_Constitution.md) §4.13 (Fail Fast), §4.7, §6.3 (never-lose-work),
§21-aligned (perceived performance); [M3 03.4 Fetching](03.4_Data_Fetching_Strategy.md), [03.11 Error &
Recovery](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md), [03.15 State Transitions](03.15_State_Transition_Philosophy.md);
frozen [States](../design/13_States.md), Design Constitution §20 + Law 13 (every state offers a way forward),
Law 6, [Feedback & Status components](../experience_design/Components/07_Feedback_Status.md).

## Architectural Decisions

### AD-1 — Non-happy-path states are first-class, standardized experiences
The frozen [States](../design/13_States.md) are delivered as **first-class, standardized** experiences — not
per-screen improvisations. The infrastructure provides **one consistent way to render each state**
(skeleton, empty, no-results, offline, permission-denied, error, partial-failure, timeout, success), so a
given state looks and behaves the same everywhere (frozen Feedback components; §4.5 consistency).

### AD-2 — States are scoped to the affected region
Each state is **region-scoped**, not global: a failed metric card shows an error in its own region while the
rest of the screen stays usable and navigation remains available ([M3 03.11 AD-1](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md);
frozen Navigation States). Loading is scoped (skeleton preserves layout), never a global blocking spinner
where a scoped one suffices (§21).

### AD-3 — Expected states are graceful; unexpected failures fail fast — both recoverable
Delivering the [M3 03.11](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md) split at the experience layer: **expected
conditions** render as calm, accessible frozen States; **unexpected failures** fail fast into a contained,
diagnostic error boundary (§4.13). **Both** offer a way forward — never a dead end (Law 13; §20).

### AD-4 — Every non-happy state guarantees recovery and preserves work
Per frozen §20 / Law 13 / Law 6: each state provides a **clear next step** (retry, refine/broaden, reconnect,
validate access, re-authenticate) and **never discards in-progress research** — Resume Session restores
context ([03.11 AD-5](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md), [04.8](04.8_Performance_Architecture.md)). No
state traps the user or loses work.

### AD-5 — Perceived performance is designed into waiting
Waiting is delivered as **productive, alive** experience (§21; frozen §5–6): **skeletons that preserve
layout**, **progressive reveal** (summary before detail), **streaming** for AI, and progress that shows
advancement — never a frozen blank. This turns waiting into visible progress ([04.8](04.8_Performance_Architecture.md)).

### AD-6 — State experiences are accessible and honest
Every state is **accessible** ([04.5](04.5_Accessibility_Architecture.md)): announced at the correct
politeness, keyboard-reachable recovery, non-color-only. And **honest**: partial failure is flagged (not
silently blank), stale/offline is labeled (not shown as live), AI "can't answer" is stated honestly — never
fabricated (trust-first; §4.13).

## Design Rationale

Non-happy paths are the **trust surface** of the product — where users on imperfect networks, best-effort
data, and AI operations spend real time. Delivering them as consistent, first-class, recoverable, accessible
infrastructure (rather than per-screen effort) is what makes the product feel dependable and premium under
adversity, and operationalizes the never-trap/never-lose-work laws everywhere at once.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Per-screen ad-hoc empty/error handling** — rejected: inconsistent, drift-prone, and easy to leave a dead
  end (contra §4.5, §20).
- **Global blocking loading/error screens** — rejected: one failure shouldn't blank the app; states are
  region-scoped (AD-2).
- **Treating states as edge cases** — rejected: they are frozen first-class States and the trust surface
  (§4.13).

## Trade-offs

- **Standardized state rendering vs. per-surface tailoring:** one consistent treatment reduces bespoke
  expressiveness but guarantees consistency and recovery. Accepted.
- **Fine-grained state scoping vs. simplicity:** region-scoped states add structure but contain failure and
  keep the rest usable. Accepted.

## Risks

- **Inconsistent or dead-end states** across features. *Mitigation:* standardized rendering (AD-1) + §20/Law
  13 recovery rule + Design QA.
- **Lost work on error/timeout.** *Mitigation:* AD-4 (Law 6) + [M3 03.11](03.11_Error_Boundary_and_Recovery.md).
- **Silent partial failure / stale-as-live.** *Mitigation:* honesty rule (AD-6); flag gaps, label offline.

## Future Extension Points

- New states (if design ever adds one via CR) adopt the standardized delivery (§4.14).
- Richer recovery affordances (smarter retry, reconnect UX) extend AD-4 without changing the model.
- Offline depth builds on [M3 03.5](03.5_Caching_Strategy.md)/[03.12](03.12_Offline_Synchronization_Philosophy.md).
