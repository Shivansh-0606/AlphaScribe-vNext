## Purpose

Define **how the Frontend Architecture evolves over time** — the governance philosophy for introducing new
documents, modifying architecture, approving changes, handling deprecations, maintaining backward
compatibility, preserving ADR history, and versioning. It ensures the architecture can grow and adapt for
years **without losing coherence, traceability, or trust**. Governance philosophy only — no implementation.

## Scope

**In scope:** the evolution philosophy — introducing documents, modifying/approving changes, deprecation,
backward compatibility, ADR-history preservation, and versioning. **Out of scope:** new governance
*mechanics* (owned by [05.7](05.7_Frontend_Governance.md)), and implementation.

## Architectural Principles

1. **Evolve additively; decouple progressively.** The architecture should grow by **addition behind stable
   interfaces** and become *less* coupled over time (§4.12, §4.14) — each change ideally leaves the system
   easier to change next time.
2. **Change frozen scope only through governance.** Nothing frozen changes silently; every change is a
   **CR/ADR** with CTO approval (§12) — the guard against erosion "one small decision at a time."
3. **Preserve history; never rewrite it.** Superseded decisions and documents are **retained**, not erased —
   the *why* survives (§11).
4. **Backward compatibility is a first-class concern.** Changes protect existing consumers of a stable
   interface; breaking changes are deliberate, governed, and migration-supported.

## Governance Responsibilities (evolution guidance)

### Introducing new documents
- A new architecture document is introduced when a genuine, unserved architectural concern exists (not
  speculative — §4.11). It follows the **required structure and metadata** ([05.2](05.2_Documentation_Standards.md)),
  is placed in the department directory, is **cross-referenced** into the roadmap/traceability matrices, and
  is approved and frozen via governance ([05.7](05.7_Frontend_Governance.md)).

### Modifying architecture
- A change to a frozen document is proposed as a **CR** (and, if significant, an **ADR**), reviewed against
  the Constitution and frozen upstream, **approved by the CTO**, then applied to the document **and its
  dependents together**, with a **version bump** and communication ([05.7](05.7_Frontend_Governance.md),
  [05.12](05.12_Governance_Responsibility_Matrix.md)). Frozen documents are never edited directly outside a CR.

### Approving changes
- Approval follows the **Definition of Done** (§13) and the review gates ([05.9](05.9_Architecture_Review_Checklist.md));
  the **CTO** is the approver/freeze authority for frozen scope. An approved change becomes the new baseline.

### Handling deprecations
- Deprecation follows **mark deprecated (with replacement + reason + target removal version) → migrate
  consumers → confirm no references → remove/supersede** ([05.5](05.5_Dependency_Guidelines.md),
  [05.6](05.6_Architecture_Decision_Record_Index.md)). Nothing in use is hard-removed; deprecations are
  communicated with a migration path.

### Maintaining backward compatibility
- Changes **protect existing consumers of stable interfaces** (§4.12): additive/minor changes must not break
  consumers; a **breaking change** is deliberate, requires a CR/ADR, and ships with a migration path and a
  major version bump. Compatibility is a review criterion, not an afterthought.

### Preserving ADR history
- Accepted ADRs are **never edited to reverse a decision** — a new ADR **supersedes** the old, which is
  retained as `Superseded` (§11, [05.6](05.6_Architecture_Decision_Record_Index.md)). The decision record is
  an append-only history; the *why* is never lost.

### Versioning architecture documents
- **Semantic versioning** ([05.2](05.2_Documentation_Standards.md)): **major** = substantive/breaking
  (re-review), **minor** = additive/refinement, **patch** = fixes. Related artifacts version consistently;
  the Revision History records every change with author, date, status, and description.

## Trade-offs

- **Governed evolution vs. speed of change:** requiring CR/ADR for frozen-scope changes slows change but
  protects coherence and the product's guarantees. Accepted.
- **Backward compatibility vs. clean-slate refactors:** protecting consumers constrains refactors, but
  stability at interfaces is what makes a long-lived system safe to change (§4.12). Breaking changes remain
  possible, deliberately and with migration. Accepted.
- **History preservation vs. tidiness:** retaining superseded decisions is less tidy but keeps rationale
  auditable. Accepted.

## Risks

- **Silent/ungoverned changes** eroding the architecture. *Mitigation:* Principle 2 + review gates
  ([05.9](05.9_Architecture_Review_Checklist.md), [05.7](05.7_Frontend_Governance.md)).
- **Breaking changes without migration.** *Mitigation:* backward-compatibility principle + deprecation path.
- **Lost rationale.** *Mitigation:* append-only ADR history (Principle 3).
- **Coupling creep over time.** *Mitigation:* evolve-additively / progressive-decoupling (Principle 1, §4.14).

## Future Evolution

- As the product and team grow, this strategy governs new milestones, sub-departments, and potential
  package/monorepo extraction — all additively and behind stable interfaces.
- Automated aids (link-checkers, boundary/version linting) can support the strategy without changing it.
