## Purpose

Define **internationalization (i18n) readiness**: the architectural preparations that keep the application
**open to future localization** without cost or rework — **without building localization, adding languages,
or changing any copy** (all out of scope for the MVP). The goal is that a future localization effort is
*additive*, not a refactor.

## Scope

**In scope:** architectural readiness for future localization — externalized user-facing text, centralized
locale-aware formatting, and layout/structure that does not preclude localization. **Out of scope (and
constraint-bound):** implementing localization, adding locales, translating or changing frozen copy
(Design owns copy), and altering UX/tokens. This is **readiness, not a feature**.

## Architectural Principles / Decisions

### AD-1 — Readiness, not localization (scope discipline)
The MVP is **English and single-locale** (frozen Vision/Roadmap; global markets are Future Vision). This
document adds **no localization capability, no locale switcher, and no translated content**. It only ensures
the architecture does not *preclude* localization later — a deliberate, low-cost readiness posture, not a
speculative feature build (§4.11 YAGNI).

### AD-2 — User-facing text is externalizable (no hardcoded concatenation)
User-facing strings are treated as **content, addressed by key**, not values hardcoded and concatenated in
components. Even for a single locale, this keeps copy **centralized and consistent** (aiding the frozen §15
vocabulary discipline) and makes a future locale an **added string set**, not a code change. **Sentence
composition avoids concatenation** (which does not translate); complete phrases are the unit. The **frozen
copy and vocabulary are unchanged** — this is about *where* text lives, not *what* it says (§6.5: Design owns
copy).

### AD-3 — Locale-aware formatting is centralized (critical for financial data)
Formatting of **numbers, currencies, percentages, and dates** — pervasive in an equity-research product — is
performed through a **single centralized formatting boundary**, not ad-hoc in components. Even in the MVP's
single locale this is correct practice (consistent figure formatting, one place to get currency/decimal rules
right) **and** it is the seam at which additional locales would attach (§4.12). This must **not** change how
figures are *presented* per the frozen design (tabular mono, decimal alignment, meaning-paired) — only route
their *formatting* through one boundary. *(Financial correctness and trust make this the highest-value
readiness item.)*

### AD-4 — Layout and structure do not preclude localization
The layout architecture ([04.4](04.4_Responsive_Architecture.md)) already adapts to variable content; the
readiness posture adds that layouts **tolerate text expansion/contraction** (translations change length) and
avoid assumptions that would block **bidirectional (RTL)** support later — favoring **logical (writing-mode-
aware) layout properties** over hard physical ones where practical. No RTL is built; the door is simply left
open (§4.14). This changes no current visual design.

### AD-5 — Localization interacts correctly with accessibility
Readiness respects accessibility ([04.5](04.5_Accessibility_Architecture.md)): a future locale carries a
correct language attribute, translated accessible names/labels, and locale-appropriate announcements. The
a11y harness accommodates this; nothing in the current architecture blocks it.

### AD-6 — Localization of trust-critical content is a governed future decision
Because the product is trust-first, **how** financial/legal/AI content would be localized (translation
quality, source-language fidelity, avoiding mistranslation of figures or claims) is a **product/design
decision for a future localization effort**, via CR — not presumed here. Readiness prepares the *mechanics*
(externalized strings, centralized formatting), not the *policy*.

| Concern | Owner |
|---------|-------|
| Copy content and frozen vocabulary (what text says) | **Design** (frozen; §6.5, §15) |
| Externalized-string structure, centralized formatting boundary, localization-tolerant layout | **Frontend Architecture** |

| Deciding to localize, and localization policy for trust-critical content | **Product/CTO** (future CR) |

## Design Rationale

A little readiness now (externalized strings, one formatting boundary, localization-tolerant layout) costs
almost nothing and prevents an expensive future refactor — the classic asymmetry that makes i18n readiness
worthwhile even when localization is not planned. Centralizing figure/date/currency formatting is
independently valuable for a financial product's consistency and correctness. Crucially, this posture adds no
feature, changes no copy or tokens, and keeps localization **policy** a governed future decision — respecting
both YAGNI (§4.11) and the frozen scope.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Building localization now (i18n framework, locales)** — rejected: out of frozen scope; speculative build
  (§4.11); would be a feature CR.
- **Ignoring i18n entirely (hardcoded strings, ad-hoc formatting)** — rejected: cheap now but forces a costly
  refactor later, and hurts figure-formatting consistency even in one locale.
- **Localizing trust-critical content mechanically** — rejected as a decision to make now: it is a governed,
  quality-sensitive future policy (AD-6).

## Trade-offs

- **Readiness effort vs. present simplicity:** externalizing strings and centralizing formatting is slightly
  more ceremony than inline literals, but pays for itself in consistency now and avoids a refactor later.
  Accepted as low-cost, high-option-value.
- **Logical layout properties vs. familiarity:** favoring writing-mode-aware properties is marginally less
  familiar but keeps RTL open at no visual cost. Accepted.

## Risks

- **Scope creep into building localization.** *Mitigation:* AD-1 — readiness only; localization is a future CR.
- **Hardcoded strings/formatting accreting** despite readiness. *Mitigation:* externalized-string +
  centralized-formatting conventions in review; lint where feasible.
- **Changing frozen copy under the guise of externalization.** *Mitigation:* §6.5/§15 — Design owns copy;
  externalization moves text, never edits it.

## Future Extension Points

- **Full localization** (locales, translations, locale switcher, RTL) builds additively on this readiness via
  a product CR (Future Vision "Global Markets") — no refactor of externalized strings/formatting (§4.14).
- **Multi-currency/locale financial formatting** extends the centralized formatting boundary.
- **Locale-aware SEO** for public surfaces extends routing ([02.3](02.3_Routing_Architecture.md)) when
  localization is adopted.
